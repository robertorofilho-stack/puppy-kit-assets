Questions

I can find free puppy checklists everywhere. Why pay for this one?

Because they're answering a different question.

Free material is good at tracking. It gives you a grid: when he ate, when he went out, how long he slept. That's genuinely useful, and if a grid is all you need, use a free one — that part of the job has been solved.

The grid doesn't tell you what any of it means. It's 11:40 p.m., he hasn't eaten since morning, he's been quiet for two hours, and the page you printed has a box for it but no line about it. So you go to a search engine, and a search engine will show you a fatal outcome and a nothing-burger on the same screen.

This guide was built for that specific minute. Every one of the 14 days has a line between what gets a phone call in the morning and what doesn't wait — and a short, plain list of the things that don't wait, from published veterinary guidance, cited where it's used.

It won't replace your vet, and it isn't meant to. What it does is make the call shorter and more useful: you'll know what you saw, when it started, and whether you should be dialing now or at 8 a.m.
